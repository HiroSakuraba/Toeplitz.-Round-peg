# Toeplitz square-peg research

**Research program:** Benjamin John Schulz (GitHub: HiroSakuraba)  
**Status as of 2026-11-20:** Research in progress. Exact square-census and relative-degree theorems have been certified for selected polygonal families; the general square-peg conjecture for arbitrary Jordan curves is **not proved** here.

The [Toeplitz square-peg problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inscribed_square_problem) asks whether every Jordan curve contains four distinct vertices of a nondegenerate Euclidean square. This is a versioned research archive of constructive half-square approaches, local certificates, geometric obstructions, analytic energy methods, and supporting computation.

## Latest certified milestones (20 November 2026)

- **[v265: generic exact irrational vertex-gluing resolver](research/v265-generic-irrational-vertex-gluing.md)**: derives a shared algebraic contact polynomial from two adjacent-edge square charts, verifies full geometric identity at a genuine polygon vertex, opposite local degrees and a uniform zero-free outer kink boundary. Combined with a complete v264 atlas, it resolves a single eligible irrational pair-death event, giving the full 3 → 2 → 1 ledger. New tests include a genuinely perturbed nonsimilar pentagon with a different event polynomial. Unsupported singularities remain inconclusive.
- **[v263: full all-real-time moving-pentagon square census](research/v263-complete-all-real-time-moving-pentagon-census.md)**: exact 70-chart exhaustion proves **3 → 2 → 1** positive inscribed square orbits across a unique irrational collision, including the singular vertex event. The selected v262 quotient atlas contains every positive square of that *particular* moving pentagon throughout the narrow certified interval.
- **[v264: reusable exact convex-polygon event atlas](research/v264-reusable-convex-square-event-atlas.md)**: accepts arbitrary rational-affine strictly convex polygon families, compresses certified collinear edge subdivisions, computes time-uniform positive square-size bounds, and exhausts all closed-edge configurations. Independently isolates the v254 algebraic event and certifies event-free chamber orbit counts. **Irrational event slices are not inferred from neighboring samples**; they require a separate event theorem.
- **[v262: C4 quotient-relative mod-2 degree](research/v262-cyclic-quotient-relative-parity.md)**: a selected eight-sheet atlas has parity degree one despite a genuine pair death. This is a *relative* and family-specific index, not an absolute obstruction on unrestricted square-configuration space.

**Remaining obstacle:** Neither the positive-size bound nor the relative quotient degree has been shown to persist uniformly across arbitrary \(C^0\) approximations of all Jordan curves. The unrestricted Toeplitz problem remains open.

## Scientific caveat

None of these manuscripts, finite checks, examples, or literature findings constitutes a proof of the unrestricted Toeplitz conjecture. Not every proposed intermediate implication survived scrutiny: in particular, v210 refutes a universal strict crossing-certificate extraction hypothesis while leaving the valid v209 sufficient criterion intact.

## v220: rectifiable fixed-curve collision-link obstruction

- [Proof and research audit](research/v220-fixed-rectifiable-collision-link-obstruction.md) construct **one fixed rectifiable Jordan curve** of perimeter `37/6`, formed from a rectangle with countably many disjoint square-shaped notches of size `s_n=4^(-n-1)`. Every notch is an exact gracefully inscribed square, so **even a single rectifiable curve need not have a uniform positive square-size floor**.
- Two four-point sequences on this same curve have **identical normalized successive parameter gaps** `(1/3,1/3,1/3)` and approach the same parameter collision chart point, but the residual divided by their common arclength parameter span has incompatible limits: **0** on the notch squares and **(37/18,−37/18,37/6,−37/18)** on nearby collinear controls. Thus that normalized residual **does not extend continuously on the parameter-only collision link**; retaining ambient shapes still leaves actual boundary zeros.
- [Exact rational constructor/checker](code/accumulating_notches_v220.py) and [13 regressions](tests/test_accumulating_notches_v220.py) check finite polygon simplicity, explicit square size, perimeter/area series, arclength gap ratios and the conflicting boundary limits. CI additionally runs the independent v216 polygon validator.
- **Scope:** This disproves a proposed boundary-regularity assumption, **not Toeplitz**. The constructed curve has infinitely many squares. The next approach must address collision-boundary zeros via a true relative cycle or macroscopic size selection.

## v230: irregular Jordan double spirals and a quantitative protected square

**Strategic pivot:** v223–v229 finite-polygon cobordism is now supporting exact infrastructure, not the primary frontier. ## v255: the sharp sqrt(2) Big Peg bound holds for convex curves

## v256-v257: sharp centered squares on arbitrary symmetric Jordan curves; quantitative near-squares beyond symmetry

- **[v256: theorem for *every* centrally symmetric Jordan curve](research/v256-centered-square-jordan-symmetry.md).** Let \(\gamma\) be any Jordan curve with symmetry center \(c\) in its interior. Half-turn symmetry then supplies **four exact boundary vertices forming a square centered at \(c\)**. If the enclosed domain contains a disk of radius \(r\) **centered at \(c\)**, this square has side **\(\ge\sqrt2\,r\)**. This proof requires **no convexity, star-shapedness, smoothness or polygonality**. It does *not* replace the centered disk radius with the potentially larger inradius of a nonconvex curve.
- **[v257: any Jordan curve gives two orthogonal boundary points and an approximate square under near symmetry](research/v257-near-central-symmetry.md).** Without symmetry, the same rotation intersection gives **two exact boundary points** at orthogonal directions and equal radius \(\ge r\). If the Hausdorff defect between the boundary and its half-turn is \(\delta<r/2\), then **four distinct boundary points** lie within \(\delta\) of an ideal centered square of side \(\ge\sqrt2 r\), with side-length errors \(\le2\delta\). This is *only an approximate square*, not an exact-square existence theorem on an arbitrary asymmetric curve.
- [Exact rational standard-library code](code/central_symmetry_square_v256.py) and [21 tests](tests/test_central_symmetry_square_v256.py) verify a **nonconvex, non-star-shaped 12-gon** containing a centered disk of radius 2, certify an exact inscribed centered square, then break its symmetry by a rational vertex displacement and construct four exact boundary points whose squared deviations from square corners are bounded by **\(1/320\)**. The general Jordan-curve theorems are pen-and-paper topological arguments, not consequences of finite polygon sampling.
- **v255 provenance correction:** the convex polygon large-square bound was answered publicly by **Christophe Boilley on June 12, 2026** in response to [David Bernier's question](https://math.stackexchange.com/questions/5140338/does-every-convex-polygon-have-only-large-inscribed-squares).
