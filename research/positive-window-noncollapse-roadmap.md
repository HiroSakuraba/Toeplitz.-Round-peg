# Research roadmap after v327: can the inradius keep a spectral square from collapsing?

**Objective.** Given a Jordan curve, construct from its geometry a positive side cutoff and arbitrarily fine registered polygonal approximations with odd local square degree above that cutoff. A universal constant times the inradius is the quantitative goal. A positive, independently computable curve-specific bound would already suffice for square existence. The published repository milestone is v327. The six versions below are proposed research stages.

**Status.** The first implementation tranche (v328, v329, v330) is in [v328–v330](v328-v330-windows-ellipses-spirals.md). v331–v333 remain proposals.

**First decision.** The original v324 marked spiral square can already be excluded as the upper spectral carrier at the square angle. A change in its turn count removes that strict exclusion in the limit. Use both fixtures before committing to a Floer computation. The calculation is in v330 below.

The analytic inspiration is positive localization on selected windows in *Dirichlet L-functions and the critical line*, §2, pp. 4–5. Here we localize in the angle between rectangle diagonals. The proof uses the Greene–Lobb derivative theorem and the repository's compactness argument. It needs no claim about Dirichlet zeros. [Greene–Lobb, §5, Theorem 5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17116v1#S5) · [v318–v322](v318-v322-support-free-spectral.md)

## v328. Build a window test with explicit approximation errors

Let $A$ be the area of the bounded open component, $r$ its inradius, $R=\operatorname{diam}(\Gamma)/2$ and $\theta_0=\pi/2$. Use v318's registered, area-convergent generic smooth approximants $\gamma_n$. One choice starts with images of concentric circles under an interior Riemann map, then makes smooth generic perturbations with errors tending to zero. Fix a subsequence on which the upper spectral functions converge uniformly, $\ell_n\to L$. For integer $m\ge1$ define

$$
w_m(\theta)=\sin^{2m}\theta,\qquad Z_m=\int_0^\pi w_m\,d\theta=\pi{2m\choose m}/4^m,\qquad
D_m(L)=Z_m^{-1}\int_0^\pi w_m\,dL .
$$

For generic smooth approximants, $\ell_n'$ almost everywhere equals the squared circumradius of an actual rectangle realizing the spectral value. Write $M_\Gamma(\theta)$ for the largest feasible circumradius, allowing zero. By v318, $0\le L'\le M_\Gamma^2$ almost everywhere. On $|\theta-\theta_0|<h$, the average is bounded by the supremum of $M_\Gamma^2$ there. Outside that interval its contribution is at most $\pi R^2\cos^{2m}(h)/Z_m$. Since $Z_m$ is of order $m^{-1/2}$, this tail vanishes at fixed $0<h<\pi/2$. Taking $h\downarrow0$ and using upper semicontinuity gives

$$
\limsup_{m\to\infty}D_m(L)\le M_\Gamma(\pi/2)^2 .
$$

A demonstrated lower bound $b(\Gamma)>0$ on the left therefore gives square side at least $\sqrt{2b(\Gamma)}$. The implication repackages v318. The missing input is a lower estimate generated by curve geometry.

**Deliverable and test.** Build an enclosure from certified values of $\ell_n$ on an angular mesh containing $\pi/2$. Bound each Stieltjes increment times the kernel by the minimum and maximum kernel value in its cell times the increment. Propagate endpoint uncertainties and monotonicity. Check the circle model $L=a^2\theta$, the v319 scalar plateau with $D_m\to0$, and a mesh deliberately too coarse to resolve the peak. Publish lower and upper answers, angular mesh, tail and spectral-data errors. A geometric census alone supplies no certified values of $\ell_n$.

**Limit order.** Integration by parts gives $|D_m(\ell_n)-D_m(L)|\le2\|\ell_n-L\|_\infty/Z_m$ since the kernel has total variation two. Take $n\to\infty$ at fixed $m$ first. A coupled mesh and smoothing sequence requires a justified error $o(Z_m)$ or an independent uniform argument.

## v329. Use ellipses to calibrate the proposed scale

On an ellipse with semiaxes $a\ge b>0$, opposite square vertices $c\pm u,c\pm v$ give $c^THu=c^THv=0$, where $H=\operatorname{diag}(a^{-2},b^{-2})$. Independence gives $c=0$. The two half-diagonals form a scaled orthonormal basis, so adding their ellipse equations gives $q^2=2a^2b^2/(a^2+b^2)$. The square with vertices $(\pm d,\pm d)$, $d^{-2}=a^{-2}+b^{-2}$, attains this radius.

Thus $q^2/(A/\pi)=2ab/(a^2+b^2)\to0$ along thin ellipses, while $q^2/r^2=2a^2/(a^2+b^2)$ lies in $[1,2)$. No universal positive multiple of the mean slope $A/\pi$ survives in the window. An inradius bound remains possible, and a circle gives the ceiling $c\le1$ for any universal squared-radius bound $cr^2$.

**Deliverable and test.** Use the circle and ellipses of aspect ratios 2, 8 and 32 at two overall scales. Report exact square radius, near-square geometric radius range and any certified spectral range separately. For generic $C^1$ approximants of a fixed ellipse, prove a uniform exclusion of shrinking near-square rectangles from local tangent control. Compactness should then squeeze all possible carrier radii to the known ellipse value as $\theta\to\pi/2$. This would calibrate both sides of the test without choosing a branch by eye.

## v330. Compare the original and retuned spirals

**Exclusion for the original marked square.** The v324 polygon edits a box of diameter tending to zero on the boundary of $[0,4]^2$. On the marked smooth approximations, $A_n\to16$ and $R_n^2\to8$. Subadditivity, the endpoint $\ell_n(\pi)=A_n$, and the spectral Lipschitz cap yield
$$
A_n/2\le\ell_n(\pi/2)\le\pi R_n^2/2,\qquad
\liminf_n\operatorname{dist}(\ell_n(\pi/2),A_n\mathbb Z)
\ge\min(8,16-4\pi)=16-4\pi>3.
$$
The marked square has side tending to zero and action distance tending to $19/24<1$. Its action therefore differs from the upper spectral value on all sufficiently fine marked smoothings. This proof works with either orientation or lift. The conclusion is for that square at one angle. Other small rectangles and nearby angles are still open. [v324 construction, signed cap computation and marked smoothing](v323-v327-cap-winding.md)

| Fixture | Construction | Question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Original spiral | $N=k^2$ turns at scale $1/k$ | Can the strict separation cover every small rectangle over a fixed neighborhood of $\pi/2$? |
| Retuned spiral | $N_k$ nearest integer to $(192/19)k^2$ at the same scale | Can one exclude a shrinking candidate whose limiting action meets the scalar spectral interval? |
| Fixed rough target | Accumulating disjoint v324 gadgets on one boundary, followed by smoothed truncations | Which bounds persist for a single nonrectifiable target? |

For general $N$ the tagged return integral at scale $1/k$ is $k^{-2}(19N/24-1/(384N)-1/2)$. The retuning makes its limit $8=A/2$. The edit box stays the same, so area and diameter still converge to their original limits. Both possible signs give residue 8 modulo 16. This removes the strict limiting gap. It leaves selection of the tagged rectangle undecided, and finite-stage bounds could still exclude it.

**Deliverable and test.** Extend the exact rational fixture to vary turn count independently of scale. For the original family, bound the oscillation of the boundary area primitive on arbitrary subarcs, including partial turns and subarcs crossing the attachment. Add the estimates on the two return arcs. Compare against $16-4\pi$ with explicit room for smoothing and angular error. A bound on all small candidates, strict on a fixed central interval, would activate v325 without a Floer computation. If it fails, identify the offending pair of subarcs and check whether an actual rectangle realizes it. Replay the fixed-target version separately. ## v331. Determine which rectangle realizes the spectral value

Work first on generic smoothings of the retuned spiral. For each angular cell record the parameter tags of the four contacts, a radius interval, action residue, admissible real lifts, and smoothing errors. Discard branches whose action intervals miss the permitted spectral interval. Away from collisions, intersect branch continuation with continuity and the derivative identity. At ambiguous cells retain every surviving possibility and bound the cell's maximum contribution by the Lipschitz cap and its kernel weight. Report a lower estimate only after accounting for unresolved cells.

**Decision.** If every admissible branch has $q\ge s>0$ on a fixed central window through refinement, v325 supplies positive spectral mass for that family. If both large and small branches remain, isolate a specific collision or action ambiguity. The next proof must either exclude all small possibilities geometrically or identify the filtered Floer class, for example through a representative and a matching lower bound on its spectral level. A radius cutoff requires proof that the differential respects it before it can define a quotient complex. The manuscript's approximate-frame estimate (Prop. 5.2, pp. 18–19) becomes a candidate tool only after constructing maps that detect this class and estimating their collective action. [Greene–Lobb, Floer setup](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.05179v2)

## v332. Force a contribution from the inball

Choose an interior disk of certified radius $\rho$ before looking for rectangles. For the universal bound require $\rho\ge\kappa r$ with an absolute $\kappa>0$. Seek sets of regular angles on which an independently identified simultaneous action/derivative carrier, or every possible such carrier, has $q\ge\beta\rho$. If these sets carry at least a fraction $\alpha$ of the normalized kernel weight, positivity gives $D_m(\ell_n)\ge\alpha\beta^2\rho^2$. Require this for arbitrarily large fixed $m$ and all sufficiently fine stages of one subsequence, with $\alpha,\beta>0$ independent of $n,m$. Then the bound passes to $L$. To get an absolute multiple of $r^2$, all three constants must be universal.

**First geometric test.** Suppose the disk center $x$ belongs to the convex hull of a carrier's vertices $p_i$ and has clearance at least $\rho$ from each. With $x=\sum t_i p_i$ a convex combination and $c$ the rectangle center,
$$
\rho^2\le\sum_i t_i|p_i-x|^2=q^2-|x-c|^2 .
$$
Hence this incidence would force $q\ge\rho$. For approximants allow the registration error in the clearance. Test the incidence on thin necks, asymmetric lobes and the retuned spirals, recording disk choice before enumeration. If a carrier misses the center, test how much window weight still satisfies the incidence. A failure of this sufficient condition can coexist with large squares on the same curve.

Track the v327 alternatives separately. The square-boundary spiral has an interior spectral gap. On targets where $\ell_n(\pi/2)\to A$, a proof that relies on distance from $A_n\mathbb Z$ needs a different source of positive size. A result for one approximation rule can establish the existential target. Failure of one rule cannot refute that target. [v327 dichotomy](v323-v327-cap-winding.md)

## v333. Produce odd local degree at a fixed side cutoff

Given the bound $b(\Gamma)>0$, fix a physical side cutoff $0<s<\sqrt{2b(\Gamma)}$. Use the positive-radius witness and the v321A marked repair to construct arbitrarily close registered polygons with an odd local square degree above $s$. Publish the registration error, contact neighborhoods, size lower bound throughout the isolating region, proof of a zero-free boundary, and mod-two count with angle modulo a quarter turn. The repaired polygons and isolating regions may vary by stage. Total degree above the cutoff may be even.

For a symmetric reference use v313's odd-degree-or-exact-cutoff alternative under its centered-disk hypotheses. For transport to an asymmetric target use v310/v315. Pair removable crossings and show all four vertices of every residual wall configuration approach the target at the fixed physical side. A crossing on a distant part of the homotopy gives no target witness. In conversions remember that v310 uses squared side while v313 and v315 use side.
