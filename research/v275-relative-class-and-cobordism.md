# v275 — The relative sign line on a verified rectangle graph

For a compact positive-radius rectangle graph \(G\) with a verified free fourfold cyclic labeling cover \(\widetilde G\), let \(L\) be its real sign line: shifting the corner labels once acts by \(-1\). The diagonal dot product \(f=(p_2-p_0)\cdot(p_3-p_1)\) is a section. Specify \(F\) to be the subset of flat vertices with the nonzero frame \(\tau=f|_F\). The pair \((G,F)\) has an obstruction \(w_1(L,\tau)\in H^1(G,F;\mathbb F_2)\). If the construction drops branches incident to total collision, this class is on the surviving graph and its specified flat subset.

Choosing arbitrary node lifts and a lift for each edge gives an edge bit recording whether the two endpoint frames agree after transport. Switching an interior lift changes the edge cochain by a coboundary. Normalizing the signs at the flat boundary fixes the relative trivialization. Solving these sign equations is equivalent to extending \(\tau\) to a nowhere-zero section: over each edge any two compatible nonzero values are joined inside one of the two components of the punctured line. A contradictory cycle detects odd monodromy, and a conflicting boundary pair detects opposite signs after transport along its path. If \(f\) were nowhere zero it would solve every sign equation, so each contradiction forces a square along that very cycle or path. This proof works at a graph vertex of any valence. Counting the resulting geometric square points as though they were transverse zeros would require a separate argument.

The v274 graph quotients to **two intervals**, with 170 vertices and 168 edges in total. Both have opposite transported endpoint signs, so the relative class is \((1,1)\). No branches were dropped. The two witnesses carry squared circumradius floors at least \(9/20\) along their entire paths. One passes through the vertex-contact unit square. The other passes through the rational interior square of squared side \(145/121\). A single sum over both components is zero in \(\mathbb F_2\), despite the nonzero relative class. The independent v278 deformation further distinguishes raw finite square counts from homotopy-invariant relative data.

## Conditional cobordism law

Suppose a compact surface \(W\) has boundary cut into two horizontal one-manifolds \(M_0,M_1\) and a vertical part \(B\), with the usual shared endpoints. Let a line on \(W\) extend the two sign lines, and let an everywhere nonzero frame on \(B\) restrict to the frames on \(\partial M_i\). Then the two evaluations of the relative first Stiefel–Whitney class agree. Indeed the mod-two fundamental chain of \(W\) has boundary \(M_0+M_1\) relative to \(B\). Pairing the cocycle \(w_1(L,\tau)\) with this relative boundary is zero. Transverse finite roots can represent these evaluations. A nonisolated or singular zero set needs the relative class or a justified perturbation rather than a raw root count.

This is a cobordism theorem under admissible boundary and line-extension hypotheses. A uniform geometric family of Jordan curves does not by itself supply such a surface, a line extension, or a zero-free frame on a size cutoff. Total collision has \(f=0\) and cannot use the flat frame. The polygon test establishes nonvanishing for the stated verified pair. Nonvanishing for arbitrary approximating curves remains open.
