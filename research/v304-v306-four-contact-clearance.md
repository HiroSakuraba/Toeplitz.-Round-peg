# v304–v306 — Moving centers, physical rectangle degree and a rough limit

Benjamin John Schulz · November 2026

Throughout, $k$ is the length of the second counterclockwise side divided by the first, in a labeling continued across the band $I=[99/100,100/99]$. The side can pass through a coordinate-frame tie. A continuum means a compact connected set. The degree continuation principle used below says that a continuous square system on a bounded box, with nonzero degree on one parameter slice and no lateral boundary zeros, has a continuum of zeros meeting both ends of the parameter interval. See [v302 §4, including the separation argument](v302-connected-rough-rectangle-crossing.md).

## v304. Free the four contacts on the radial example

**Theorem.** For every continuous Jordan embedding at registered Euclidean sup distance at most $\eta=10^{-5}$ from the v303 two-frequency radial curve, one continuum of actual rectangles spans $I$. Every side exceeds $0.69$. The centers are free variables determined by the contacts. No regularity beyond continuity is required of the perturbation.

Write $g(s)=r(s)e_s$, $e_s=(\cos s,\sin s)$, where
$$r(s)=[1+0.01\cos4s+0.001\cos(2s+\beta)]/2,\quad
(\cos\beta,\sin\beta)=(4/5,3/5).$$
The reference satisfies $g(s+\pi)=-g(s)$. Its radius lies between $r_-=0.4945$ and $r_+=0.5055$, with $|r'|\le0.021$, $|r''|\le0.082$, $|g''|<0.64$. For target $h$ and $\phi_0=2\arctan(1/k)$ use four source angles in this order:
$$s+a,\quad s+\phi+b,\quad s+\pi-a,\quad s+\phi+\pi-b,\qquad \phi=\phi_0+z.$$
Use the box $|a|,|b|,|z|<0.01$, $|2s+\phi_0+\beta|<\pi/6$, on one real angle lift. These four source variables vary independently. For their images $p_i$, set $U=(p_0-p_2)/2$, $V=(p_1-p_3)/2$. Impose equality of the two opposite-pair midpoints and $V=R_{\phi_0}U$. These are four real equations. At a zero they give the desired rectangle with $k=\cot(\phi_0/2)$.

**Midpoint control.** On the closed box the first reference midpoint is $a g'(s)$ with error at most $0.32a^2$, by Taylor's theorem. The second is $b g'(s+\phi)$ with error at most $0.32b^2$. Each midpoint moves by at most $\eta$ under the perturbation. Put $M=\max(|a|,|b|)$. If $|a|=M$, project the midpoint equality onto $Je_s$, where $J$ is a quarter turn. The diagonal coefficient is $r(s)$. The other coefficient has magnitude at most $L=r_+(199/19801+0.01)+0.021$, since $|\cos\phi_0|\le199/19801$. For $|b|=M$ use $Je_{s+\phi}$. Thus
$$(r_- - L-0.0064)M\le2\eta,\qquad M\le5\eta.$$
The common center has norm at most $(r_++0.021)M+0.32M^2+\eta<4\eta$.

**Angle control.** The first target half-diagonal differs from $g(s)$ by at most $\eta+0.32M^2$, using the symmetric average; the same holds for the second and $g(s+\phi)$. Hence $|g(s+\phi)-R_{\phi_0}g(s)|\le B=2\eta+0.64(5\eta)^2$. Projection perpendicular to $e_{s+\phi_0}$ gives $r_-|\sin z|\le B$, so $|z|<5\eta$ using $|\sin z|\ge0.99|z|$ on this box. The radial mismatch at the prescribed angle is therefore at most $B+0.021(5\eta)$.

Write $w=2s+\phi_0$. The exact v303 identity for this mismatch is
$$r(s+\phi_0)-r(s)=-\sin\phi_0[0.04\cos\phi_0\sin w\cos w+0.001\sin(w+\beta)].$$
At either remaining face $w+\beta=\pm\pi/6$ its magnitude is at least $(1-199/19801)(0.0005-0.02\cdot199/19801)>0.00029$, above the mismatch bound $0.000022$. All four face pairs are excluded. At a zero $|U|\ge r_- -\eta-0.32(5\eta)^2$, so the shorter side is at least $2|U|(99/100)/\sqrt{1+(99/100)^2}>0.69$.

**Degree.** At zero error the bounds force $a=b=z=0$. The bracket has one simple zero on the chosen interval by the v303 derivative bound. The full zero is regular: midpoint differentiation in $(a,b)$ gives the two independent tangent columns from the projection estimate, with zero derivatives in $(s,z)$ at $a=b=0$. In the half-diagonal equation, resolve components in the moving frame $e_{s+\phi_0},Je_{s+\phi_0}$. The transverse component is $r(s+\phi)\sin z$, with positive $z$ derivative and zero $s$ derivative at the zero. The radial component there has nonzero $s$ derivative by the simple root. Thus the full degree is odd. Straight interpolation from $g$ to $h$ preserves the bounds; intermediate maps need only be continuous. Degree continuation in $k$ proves the theorem. [v303 identity and simple-root proof](v303-finding-a-direction.md)

**An actual moving-center test.** Add $\eta|\sin(s/2)|$ to the reference radius. This positive radial Jordan curve has precisely one nonsmooth corner. Any half-turn preserving it would have to fix that corner, whereas the center of a centrally symmetric Jordan curve lies inside (the half-turn on the closed Jordan disk has a fixed point, and a half-turn centered on its boundary would induce a nonidentity orientation-preserving involution of the boundary circle with a fixed point). Thus the target has no center of symmetry. Equal radii at antipodal angles occur only on one line, since $|\sin(s/2)|=|\cos(s/2)|$ only there. No rectangle on this curve can have center at the reference origin. Every rectangle supplied by the theorem has moved its center.

## v305. Recover a physical rectangle box from a vertex list

**Certificate.** Use the cyclic rational polygon vertices as the sole input. Validate simplicity and orient counterclockwise. Enumerate nondecreasing edge quadruples (including repeats), solve the four affine counterclockwise square equations and retain distinct, on-segment solutions. Try each cyclic labeling. This search uses isolated supporting-line solutions as anchors and may give no answer. At an anchor normalize by the orientation-preserving similarity sending its first side to $(0,0),(1,0)$. Units below are lengths of that original side. For the corners $P_i=(0,0),(1,0),(1,1),(0,1)$, parameterize a physical rectangle by
$$X=(x,y,h,v),\quad p_0=(x,y),\quad u=(1+h,v),\quad p_1=p_0+u,\quad
p_3=p_0+kJu,\quad p_2=p_1+kJu.$$
This uses position and side vector in place of center and half-diagonal. They determine each other on the box.

Write $m_i$ for the anchored edge slopes. Check that inside the four windows $|p_x-P_{ix}|\le1/6$, $|p_y-P_{iy}|\le1/4$ the whole boundary consists locally of one graph spanning the horizontal interval. Clip every edge, check adjoining pieces for gaps and overlaps, and reject interfering arcs. Bound the graph's Lipschitz constant $L_i$ and its maximum deviation $d_i$ from the anchored line exactly at the clipped vertices. Let $A$ be the derivative in $X$ of the four signed vertical offsets from the reference lines at $k=1$. Define $b=1/64$ and use the physical box $\|AX\|_\infty<b$. Singular $A$ gives no certificate.

The replay computes $m=b\|A^{-1}\|_\infty$ and $D=(2+1/t)m+1/t-1$, $t=99/100$. Every corner coordinate moves at most $D$ from its anchor for $k\in I$. In row $i=0,1$ changing the ratio makes no difference. In rows $2,3$ it costs at most $e_i=(1/t-1)(1+m+|m_i|m)$ in vertical offset. On any lateral face at least one corresponding graph residual has the sign of its active row of $AX$ and magnitude at least $b-d_i-e_i$. A Euclidean lower bound on the distance of that corner from the whole polygon is therefore
$$C=\min\{1/6-D,1/4-D,\min_i (b-d_i-e_i)/(1+L_i)\}.$$
For boundary portions outside a window use the first two terms. For the local graph use the Lipschitz inequality. The verifier requires $C>0$ and $m<1$. This gives the requested physical signed-distance clearance; inside a window the signed vertical offset has the same zero set as signed distance, with constant sign convention up to reversal per row. The straight residual homotopy from $AX$ to the graph equations retains the active sign, hence degree modulo two is one. Continuation across $I$ gives one spanning continuum; the shorter side is at least $t(1-m)$. The four windows and four generating edge labels are discovered by the search. Subsequent corners may cross polygon vertices.

**Transfer to independently moved boundary arcs.** A certificate also applies to any Jordan embedding at registered Euclidean sup distance strictly less than $C$ in normalized units, even if its arcs fold back and cease to be graphs. Use the signed distance of the target (negative inside), with a fixed sign per row that matches the base vertical offset. On a box face, its active corner is at least $C$ from the base boundary. Straight interpolation of the registered curves avoids that corner, so the winding number about it stays fixed. Consequently the target signed distance has the same active sign as the base, even if some intermediate maps self-intersect. Linear interpolation between the four base graph residuals and these target signed distances has no boundary zero. A target zero places all four actual corners on the target curve. The degree, spanning conclusion and physical side floor transfer. The CLI reports squared clearance and squared side floor in input units to avoid taking an irrational square root or inadvertently rescaling across refinements.

**Decisive example.** The input is the 13-vertex polygon in the code, transformed by $(x,y)\mapsto(x-y,x+y)$. It is nonconvex, for example the inward kink on its lower arc at $(1/4,1/128)$ in normalized coordinates. The search returns the square with origin $(0,0)$ and first side vector $(1,1)$ in the supplied frame. Its zero-based edge labels are $(1,4,8,10)$. The clearance is $73/22176$ and the shorter-side floor is $10659/11200$ in normalized units. In the first window the lower graph is $0$ at $x\le0$ and $x/32$ at $x\ge0$. The other three graphs are straight. At either endpoint solve both resulting affine systems and discard the solution on the wrong side of the kink. The record gives the unique admissible endpoint in the box in each case. Their $x$ coordinates have opposite signs, as do their $v$ coordinates. Thus on the same continuum the first corner passes through the supporting-edge junction and the first side passes through $45^\circ$ in the supplied frame. At $k=1$ both linear pieces have only the common solution $X=0$ (their matrices are nonsingular). [Exact endpoint coordinates](../results/v304-v306/crossings.json)

## v306. A common positive size on a thin rough limit

Use the normalized polygon from v305, with two disjoint horizontal windows per long boundary arc. At corner $i$, write $u$ for the horizontal coordinate, $u_i=P_{ix}$ and $s$ for $u$ clamped to $[u_i-1/6,u_i+1/6]$. Add this height to the original graph locally:
$$\delta_{i,N}(u)= \lambda_i\chi_i(u)(1+W_N(s))/2048,\qquad
W_N(s)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}2^{-j}\,2\operatorname{dist}(4^j s,\mathbb Z),$$
where $\lambda=(1,-3/4,1/2,-1/4)$. The cutoff equals one on the inner window and decreases linearly to zero at distance $1/4$. Clamping the argument before tapering makes every finite curve piecewise linear. All four old contacts leave the curve. The displacement is at most $1/1024$ and the entire remaining tail at depth $N$ costs at most $2^{-N}/2048$. The two graphs stay separated on the modified supports and join their original vertical ends, giving Jordan polygons and a Jordan limit.

**Transfer.** The projection of every rectangle corner in the certified box stays in its inner window. The worst vertical graph change along the straight homotopy is at most $1/1024<C$. Consequently the same active-row signs and odd degree hold for every finite depth and directly for the continuous limit. For each of these curves there is one continuum spanning $I$ with shorter side at least $10659/11200>0.95$ and half-diagonal at least $(10659/11200)/\sqrt2$. Alternately take a Hausdorff-convergent subsequence of the spanning continua in the common compact box times $I$. Connectedness and both endpoint fibers survive and the common side floor excludes collapse. This requires no matching of individual rectangles from one depth to the next.

**Roughness at the contacts.** On any interior subinterval of a plateau, consider cells of length $4^{-n}$ lying in the subinterval on one affine piece of the reference graph. At their ends and midpoints every later term vanishes, the earlier terms are affine, and the $n$th term contributes a midpoint excess of $2^{-n}$ over the mean of the endpoints. The variation on each cell is at least twice the absolute scaled excess. There are order $4^n$ cells, giving unbounded variation on every such subinterval. Each selected corner therefore lies on an arc of infinite length in every neighborhood. The smooth sides elsewhere cannot account for the four selected contacts.

**Outside the thin-annulus case.** All curves have diameter at least 9 and fit in a strip of width less than 1.26. For any interior center the outer radius is at least half the diameter and the inner clearance at most half the strip width. The ratio exceeds $9/1.26>1+\sqrt2$. This rules out the nonzero-winding thin-annulus criterion on these inputs. [Matschke, survey, Theorem 5](https://www.math.u-bordeaux.fr/~bmatschke/SurveyOnSquarePeg_extension14.pdf)

**Shrinkout control.** The selected v272 bump square on an otherwise unit-sized curve has physical side $1/n$ and half-diagonal $1/(n\sqrt2)$. Normalizing each selected square to unit side would hide the collapse. In the v306 proof the physical scale is set once by the coarse polygon. No certificate is re-normalized as depth increases. [v272 construction](v272-degeneration-trichotomy-and-shrinkout.md)

## Reproduction and scope

The [radial margins and twelve rational samples](../results/v304-v306/radial-and-rough.json) record the analytic bounds and the physical rectangle parameters. Run `python code/radial_and_rough_v304_v306.py --check`, `python code/rectangle_clearance_v305.py --check` and `python -m unittest discover -s tests -p 'test_rectangle_clearance_v304_v306.py' -v`. Supply an unmarked cyclic JSON array of rational coordinate pairs to `python code/rectangle_clearance_v305.py --polygon polygon.json` to search another polygon. The tests check rational radial inequalities, reordered polygon input, an interfering boundary arc, twelve exact rough rectangles on four finite refinements, the infinite-tail estimates at sample points, and the shrinkout scale. The analytic estimates and continuation proofs above establish the statements for all ratios and depths. The search is sufficient and incomplete. A curve-specific positive floor obtained directly for an arbitrary Jordan curve remains open.
