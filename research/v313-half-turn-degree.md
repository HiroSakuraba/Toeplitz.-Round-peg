# v313. Half-turn cancellation and a positive-scale window alternative

Benjamin John Schulz · November 2026

Let a Jordan curve be invariant under the half-turn about an interior point $O$, and let its interior contain the open disk of radius $r$ about $O$. For **each** side cutoff $0<s<\sqrt2 r$, either the curve has a square of side exactly $s$, or its geometric squares above $s$ have degree **one modulo two**. The latter conclusion survives sufficiently small arbitrary registered Jordan perturbations. The degree counts each physical square once and applies to a continuum of squares. The [v256 rotation argument](v256-centered-square-jordan-symmetry.md) already gives a centered square on the symmetric curve. Here the additional information is the filtered degree, its stability when symmetry is lost, and how it forces one of the [v312 window outcomes](v312-two-arc-winding.md).

## Why crossings at the cutoff can be allowed

Use the [v307 square bundle](v307-convex-shell-square-gap.md). Its coordinates are center $C$, side length $\ell>0$, and angle modulo a quarter turn. The four evaluations of a continuous defining function at the square vertices form a rank-four section; changing the first vertex cyclically permutes them. Use negative defining functions on the interior and positive ones outside. For a zero-free cutoff, truncate the domain $\ell\ge s$ at large center and size bounds. The mod-two degree on it is well defined. Translating $O$ to the origin, the half-turn acts by $C\mapsto-C$, leaving $\ell$ and the angle class fixed. On evaluation vectors its action is a shift by two entries.

Start with a centered ellipse of distinct semiaxes $2r,r$ and interpolate its even defining function to the even signed distance of the target. All point zeros stay in a common large disk. At every time the defining function is strictly negative on the circle $|x|=s/\sqrt2$, since the disk's interior is inside both reference shapes. Consequently **no fixed point of the half-turn on the cutoff face can be a section zero**. A square fixed by the half-turn has center zero, and all its vertices would lie on that circle. Intermediate sections may have off-center cutoff zeros.

Here is the parity accounting even for nontransverse or nonisolated crossings. The compact zero set on the cutoff-face times the time interval stays away from the fixed locus and the time endpoints, assuming that the final cutoff is zero-free. In an invariant neighborhood of this zero set the involution acts freely. Descend the section and its bundle to the quotient there, perturb transversely with support away from the neighborhood boundary, and lift. Every resulting cutoff zero occurs in a pair. This relative perturbation extends to a collar of the face in the full homotopy. Complete a transverse perturbation on its interior and time faces, keeping the side-face data and avoiding the zero-free seams. The resulting compact one-manifold of zeros has even total boundary count. Its outer faces have no zeros, and the cutoff contributes even parity. Thus the two time faces have equal mod-two degree. The ellipse has one regular geometric square, of side strictly above $s$ (the ellipse calculation in v307). This proves the assertion. Interior zeros need no equivariant transversality. The argument also applies to an even continuous defining field with bounded zeros, positive outside a large disk and negative on the disk in question, without requiring its zero set to be a Jordan curve.

**Stability or a whole interval of sizes.** Let $0<a<\sqrt2r$. If even one size $s$ in $(a,\sqrt2r)$ is missing on the symmetric reference, every sufficiently close registered Jordan curve has a square of side greater than $s$. Indeed, for all small enough $\epsilon$ the closed $\epsilon$-tube of the reference contains no square of side exactly $s$. Otherwise compactness of centers and angles would give one on the reference. Signed-distance interpolation to a registered perturbation of size $\epsilon$ has all its point zeros inside this tube: outside it, straight interpolation of the parametrizations preserves winding and hence inside/outside status. The degree transfers. Conversely, if a sequence converging to the symmetric reference has largest-square sides with liminf at most $a$ (use zero for an empty spectrum), the reference realizes **every** size in $(a,\sqrt2r)$. In particular empty interior of the reference size spectrum below $\sqrt2r$ suffices for a stable lower bound arbitrarily close to $\sqrt2r$. The neighborhoods may depend on the chosen lower bound.

**A one-scale test on a nonsymmetric target.** Let $f$ be its signed distance, still negative on the disk. For every side-$s$ square $X$ form $A=(f(x_j))$ and $B=(f(-x_j))$ using corresponding vertices. If $A$ vanishes, the cutoff itself is attained. Otherwise a sufficient condition for odd degree above $s$ is
$$
0\notin [A,(A+B)/2]\quad\text{for every such }X.
$$
Interpolate $f$ to its even part; the test precisely excludes a boundary zero along this homotopy. Apply the even-field theorem to the midpoint and transfer back. For instance it suffices that each trial square have one corner where $f(x_j)$ and $f(-x_j)$ have the same strict sign. Conversely a zero-free cutoff of **even** degree forces an antiparallel residual pair $B=-\lambda A$, $\lambda\ge1$, somewhere on that scale. This conclusion concerns four values at once, not just the asymmetry at one boundary point. The disk condition by itself does not imply the test.

## From geometric degree to an ordinary two-arc window

**Grid theorem.** On any Jordan curve suppose side $s>0$ is absent and the geometric degree above $s$ is odd. Use any fixed parametrization and nested dyadic partitions of the source circle. At some finite level there are two closed dyadic intervals with image distance **greater than $s$** for which either an exact square lies on the parameter-window perimeter, or the v312 winding inside the window is odd. Different levels are allowed. This also applies after transfer to a nonsymmetric curve. On the circle the first alternative occurs: each marked point starts an inscribed square and refining the two side intervals separates them. The argument never requires the circle's residual winding to be nonzero.

**Proof.** A large square with any vertex at any dyadic mark gives the first alternative. Designate that vertex the first endpoint of a positively oriented side and refine both side windows until their images have distance greater than $s$. Hence suppose every vertex of every large square misses all dyadic marks. The large geometric zero set is compact by the zero-free cutoff. Choose a coarse dyadic mesh with all image diameters smaller than $s/4$. Every large square uses four distinct coarse intervals. Designate its vertex with smallest interval index as the start of its positively oriented side. The choice is locally constant as a choice of labeling on the zero set, because no vertex hits a mark. It selects one ordered-side lift per geometric square.

In the space of oriented sides the selected compact set and its three other cyclic lifts are disjoint. Excision in disjoint neighborhoods of the lifts identifies the unlabelled degree with the degree on the selected lift: forgetting the label is a homeomorphism there after shrinking to one neighborhood disjoint from its translates. In the side parameter torus the selected pairs also form a compact set separated from all other zero pairs, including the diagonal and smaller squares. This follows from uniqueness of completion of an oriented side and compactness of the closed zero-pair set; the selected set is relatively open there by the size gap and coarse label test.

For clarity, the local degree in four variables on a lift equals modulo two the v312 two-variable degree. Use $Q,B$ as independent planar coordinates; the other corners are $P=Q+R(B-Q)$, $D=B+R(B-Q)$. The first two equations require $Q,B\in\Gamma$. In a topological collar of each arc (obtained by the planar Schoenflies theorem), their signed distances can be replaced by signed transverse coordinates using interpolation, with the same zero set. Contract the two remaining residuals in the transverse variables to their values on the arcs. Zeros during the contraction still require both transverse coordinates to vanish. The collar exists for arbitrary planar Jordan embeddings; see [Cairns, Proceedings of the AMS (1951)](https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~mlyubich/Archive/Topology/Jordan.pdf). The product rule for degree gives precisely the degree of $(f(P),f(D))$ on the side window. These reductions can equally be made on a union of isolating cells by excision.

Take all sufficiently fine dyadic product cells meeting the selected compact set. Their closures miss the other zero pairs, lie where side separation exceeds $s$, and carry no zeros on their seams. Compactness and uniform continuity allow this choice simultaneously. Their winding parities sum to the geometric degree one. At least one cell is odd. The proof supplies eventual termination for a sequence of exact level searches under these hypotheses, without a universal mesh-depth estimate.

**Exact polygonal search.** On a perimeter sweep one of $Q,B$ is fixed and the other moves linearly on an edge. Each completed vertex moves on a segment. Intersect both trajectories with every boundary edge, retaining the full closed interval of times for collinear overlap. Sort the event times and probe these and the open-interval midpoints. A simultaneous contact returns the exact square. Otherwise replace each residual vector by its two signs at each probe. Between successive probes at least one sign is strictly constant, so the corresponding portion of the residual path and the straight sign-vector segment lie in the same open half-plane and are homotopic with the endpoint replacements. The resulting closed rational walk has the original winding. This handles tangencies, polygon vertices and overlaps which the generic ray counter of v312 declines. For a level search we first require the exact minimum distance between the two polygonal subarcs to exceed $s$.

## A matched pair of genuine crossings, and a single unpaired crossing

Start with $[-4,4]\times[-1,1]$. Attach a polygonal protrusion at the top, centered at $(1,1)$, and its half-turn at the bottom, centered at $(-1,-1)$. The normalized profile goes from left to right through
$$
(-1,0),\ (-1/8,0),\ (-3/8,1/2),\ (7/16,17/32),\ (1/8,0),\ (1,0).
$$
Rescale around the attachment point by $\lambda\in[9/10,1]$. The first and last segments are the horizontal boundary outside the mouth. The two scales may move independently. Every loop is Jordan and contains the open unit disk.

At side $s=2/5$, every square must use a protrusion: on the unmodified rectangle all squares have side two (see v307). A cutoff square touching a protrusion cannot reach a vertical side or the opposite horizontal wall, by its diameter $\sqrt2s$. It is contained in that protrusion and its supporting horizontal line. Its horizontal span equals its vertical span, at most $17\lambda/32$. Thus all its contacts are within the normalized profile's $[-1,1]$ range since $7/16+17/32=31/32<1$. This excludes mixed or omitted events.

The exact affine contact equations on the five normalized edges give two squares, both regular with strictly interior tags, with squared sides
$$
1386334197/9194892100\quad\text{and}\quad 533/2890.
$$
The replay tries all edge tuples modulo cyclic resetting and excludes every feasible singular chart of positive size by testing all vertices of its tag polytope. Convexity bounds the squared side on the polytope by its maximum on those vertices. After scaling, only the larger normalized square crosses the cutoff, at $\lambda^2=2312/2665$. The derivative of its squared side in $\lambda$ is positive. Its invertible four-contact chart and this nonzero derivative give one transverse time/size-wall zero per moving protrusion.

| Deformation | Cutoff crossings | Degree above $2/5$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Both scales $9/10\to1$ together | Two, at distinct reflected squares at the same time | $1\to1$ |
| Upper scale $9/10\to1$, lower fixed at $9/10$ | One | $1\to0$ |

The second endpoint still has side-two squares on the long rectangle. Even degree allows their presence. More sharply, split the two scales just above and just below the critical scale: compared to a symmetric start just below the critical scale, only the upper square crosses. Thus arbitrarily small symmetry defects can give even degree at this fixed cutoff near a symmetric **wall event**. This explains the zero-free qualification in the stability statement.

## Quantitative nonsymmetric neighborhood and independent window checks

At the symmetric endpoint $\lambda=1$, the exact checker also excludes all squares with sides in the entire interval $[7/5,8/5]$ from the closed coordinate-sup tube of halfwidth $1/32$. The proof exhausts all ordered pairs of boundary edges, recursively splitting their parameter rectangles. For projected endpoints $Q,B$ the two coordinate ranges of their actual side vector expand by $2\epsilon$. If the resulting squared norm range misses the tested interval the entire cell is discarded. Otherwise compare the predicted boxes for $Q+R(B-Q)$ and $B+R(B-Q)$ to all possible boundary segments after expansion by $4\epsilon$ (three endpoint-coordinate errors and one error for the tested contact). Disjoint axis projections or disjoint segment-normal projections exclude a segment. Subdivision covers both closed child cells. The large-gap check closes after 702 leaves, with maximum depth 12. A separate small-cutoff run certifies $2/5$ at halfwidth $1/8192$.

Since $7/5<\sqrt2$, the symmetric theorem gives odd degree above $7/5$. Signed-distance interpolation and the large tube gap now give a square of side **strictly greater than $8/5$** on every registered Jordan perturbation of coordinate sup size at most **$1/32$**, with no remaining symmetry requirement. The dyadic grid theorem applies on each such target at $8/5$ as well. This latter claim uses the absence of the whole closed interval to identify the two degrees.

On the exact reference the level-3 scan returns a perimeter square of side two. With the edge-uniform parametrization, cells 343 and 101 at level 10 instead have winding $+1$ and squared separation $11515205/67108864>(2/5)^2$. An independent generic-ray calculation on a second pair of windows detects the regular square of squared side $533/2890$.

For arbitrary curves the missing step is to control unpaired cutoff crossings or otherwise force positive-scale geometric degree or a boundary square. The split deformation gives a concrete obstruction to assuming that an inball or a small symmetry defect alone provides that control. It is an obstruction to the parity argument, not an example of collapse of all squares.
