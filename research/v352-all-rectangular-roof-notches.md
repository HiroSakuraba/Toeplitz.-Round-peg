# v352. The disk bound for every rectangular roof notch

Remove one open rectangular bite from the roof of a rectangle, keeping positive width and height for both towers and the bottom strip. For every strict cutoff below $\sqrt2 r$, where $r$ is the inradius, every sufficiently close registered Jordan redraw has an inscribed square above the cutoff. The redraw may be nonpolygonal.

**Reduction.** Reflect to put the wider tower on the left. Write $A\ge d$ for the tower widths, $n$ for the notch width, $h$ for the floor height, $W=A+n+d$ for the outer width and $H>h$ for the outer height. Put $B=A+n$. [v351](v351-one-rectangular-roof-notch.md) handles $H>W$ and $A\ge H$. For $W>H>A$, its sliding rectangle test succeeds exactly when $W-H<d$, i.e. $H>B$. Its comparison L transfers when $\min(H,A+h)<B$, in particular if $H<B$ or $h<n$. Only $H=B$ with $h\ge n$, and the square box $H=W$, need further work. We prove a robust floor of $H/\sqrt2$ in both cases. The enclosing strip gives $2r\le H$.

## The collision at H = B

Select the axis squares of side $H$ with bottom left $(s,0)$, $0\le s\le d$. Both upper corners are on the roof. At $s=0$, the upper right corner hits the right notch wall $x=B$. Continue the square through this contact using the four points
$$
(u,0),\quad(H,u),\quad(H-u,H),\quad(0,H-u),
\qquad \max(h,n)\le u\le H .
$$
Use the cyclic shift that matches the original labeling at $u=H$. These are exactly the permitted positions on this branch: the right notch wall starts at $h$ and the left roof ends at $A$. When $d=A$, attach the reflected branch at $s=d$, reversing the order of reflected vertices to preserve orientation and matching the labels there. When $d<A$ that end has the unchanged corner geometry of the enclosing rectangle. The family is an interval with a continuous single labeling; distinct slide positions or nonaxis branch members give distinct geometric squares (the left branch touches the left wall and stays within $x\le H<W$; the reflected branch stays away from the left wall). Every member spans height $H$, hence has side at least $H/\sqrt2$.

**Local isolation off the axis.** On the left branch the first and fourth contacts near a nonaxis member stay inside the bottom and left edges. Write them as $(u,0),(0,v)$; the other two points are $(t,u),(v,t)$ with $t=u+v$. On the marked wall or floor, $t\le B$ and $u\ge h$. On the marked roof or inner left wall, either $t=H$ or $v=A$. If $t<H=B$, then $v=A$ gives $u<n\le h$, impossible. This includes the double corner at $h=n$. At $t=H$ the exact interval above results. Reflection handles the other branch.

**At the join.** Near the axis square at $s=0$, label its corners bottom left, bottom right, upper right, upper left. The bottom left is $(x,y)$ with $x,y\ge0$, $xy=0$; bottom right stays in the bottom interior. Write the first side vector as $(D,-y)$. Upper left is $(x+y,D+y)$ on the left wall or roof; upper right is $(x+D+y,D)$ on the roof to the right of $B$ or on the notch wall at $B$. If $y>0$, then $x=0$, $D=H-y$, and upper right is $(H,H-y)$, giving exactly the rotating branch. If $y=0,x>0$, then $D=H$, giving ordinary sliding. If $x=y=0$, upper right is $(D,D)$, forcing $D=H$. At a slide interior the upper and lower contacts are in the straight supports and force an axis square. At an unchanged end the rectangle isolation of [v340](v339-v340-end-wall-selection.md) applies. These checks isolate the entire labeled family.

**Computing its degree.** Increase the height to $H'=H+\epsilon<W$, keeping $B$ fixed. If $h=n$, increase $h$ slightly as well so that the new floor $h'>n$; otherwise keep $h'=h$. Register boundaries edge by edge. We enumerate the perturbed zeros locally to avoid assuming that a nearby odd component automatically gives odd total degree.

1. Near a nonaxis branch member the same contact constraints give $t\le B<H'$, hence $v=A$ and $u\le n<h'$. No such solution remains.
2. At the left join the same lower and upper-left equations hold with $H'$ in place of $H$. For $y>0$ the upper right would be $(H',H'-y)$ on the notch wall $x=B$, impossible. For $y=0,x>0$ we get the ordinary sliding interval. For $x=y=0$ there are two possibilities: $D=H'$ on the roof (its sliding endpoint), or $D=B$ on the notch wall. This last isolated zero has degree zero. Its upper-left contact lies in the left wall interior at height $B<H'$. Push just that small subarc to the left near this contact. The completion requires its abscissa to equal $x+y\ge0$, so a tiny displacement removes the local zero. Choose the neighborhood with that contact inside the displaced portion, disjoint from the other three contact arcs. The reflected join, when present, gives the same zero contribution.
3. The remaining zeros form the whole height-$H'$ sliding family, with $0\le s\le W-H'$. Its contact bands are strictly clear of the notch since $H'>B$ and $W-H'<d$. It has odd degree by v351. All these squares converge into the original isolator as $\epsilon\downarrow0$. Conversely any sequence of zeros in a sufficiently small closed isolator has a limit in the original family, so the local classifications above exhaust the perturbed zeros there.

Additivity and homotopy invariance give odd degree at the collision. Shrink the isolator around the family to protect any strict side cutoff below its minimum, then use uniform continuity of the four-point residual under registered redraws.

## The square box H = W

Let $S=W=H$. In the uncut box the labeled axis square continues by rotation with points $(u,0),(S,u),(S-u,S),(0,S-u)$, $0\le u\le S$. The notch deletes the open interval for which the top contact $S-u$ lies in $(A,B)$. Join the two remaining intervals at the axis square using cyclic relabeling at the seam. This gives one compact labeled interval: the break from the notch has positive length, so the other cyclic lifts are disjoint. Every square has span $S$ and side at least $S/\sqrt2$.

At a nonaxis member the bottom and left contacts are in their edge interiors and right contact forces $u+v=S$. The last contact has height $S$, so at a notch corner it cannot travel down the wall. These are all nearby solutions. Around the axis member all four corners have the local geometry of the uncut box. For clarity, any square near that member with four corners on the box boundary either has a contact on each side, giving the rotation equations, or has two corners on one side. In the latter case they must be adjacent, the square is axis-aligned, and four boundary contacts near the axis member force it to span a full dimension. This also describes the unequal rectangle locally.

Increase just the outer width to $S+\epsilon$ by moving its right wall. The notch stays fixed. No square remains near any nonaxis member: bottom and left anchors and the right-wall contact would put the fourth point at height $S+\epsilon>S$. Near the axis member only uncut rectangle contacts occur. With unequal spans four different sides cannot support a nonaxis square (the two box spans of a square are equal); the surviving zeros are the height-$S$ sliding squares. Their traces avoid the notch and have odd degree by v340. The same isolator and homotopy argument proves the required robust bound in the square box. This finishes all real parameter cases.
